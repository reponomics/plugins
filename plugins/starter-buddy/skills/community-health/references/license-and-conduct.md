# License and code-of-conduct guidance

Use existing policies and the maintainer's choices. A brief answer may be enough; if the user wants to explore options, help them do so. Either topic can wait while the rest of the setup proceeds.

## License

Establish the selected license's name and version or SPDX identifier, plus any copyright fields needed for its text. When asked, share general facts about open source licenses, explain relevant differences, and help the user understand the options. [Choose a License](https://choosealicense.com/) is an accessible starting point. The maintainer makes the selection before it is applied.

Check existing licensing before adding or replacing text. If repository evidence reveals conflicting terms or unclear ownership, explain the specific uncertainty and resolve it with the maintainer; unrelated setup can continue. Preserve separately licensed material and its notices.

Fetch the selected text from the license steward, the [Open Source Initiative license list](https://opensource.org/licenses/), or GitHub's [license API](https://docs.github.com/en/rest/licenses/licenses). Preserve the canonical body and required notices, fill its intended fields, and put a new repository license at the root. If licensing is deferred, leave the file unchanged or absent and note the remaining decision.

## Code of conduct

Explain the practical commitment: someone will receive private reports and take responsibility for handling them. If adoption is wanted, establish the source, version, any customization, and the responsible person or team and reporting contact. Use details already provided, including permission to publish contact information.

Useful sources include:

- GitHub's [code-of-conduct templates and API](https://docs.github.com/en/rest/codes-of-conduct).
- The [Contributor Covenant builder](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/) for text, translations, and enforcement customization.
- The Open Source Guides' [Your Code of Conduct](https://opensource.guide/code-of-conduct/) for adoption and enforcement guidance.

Fetch the chosen version and follow its attribution and customization terms. Review substantive changes with the maintainer so the policy reflects the responsibilities they intend to accept. Sensitive conduct reports need a private route approved for publication.

If the maintainer is still deciding, help compare policies or prepare a clearly identified draft when useful. Finalize the policy when its reporting route and responsibilities are settled; the other community files can be delivered independently.
